# Audit of 2026-11-07: agents and fulfillers

You said the panel was not made for agents and fulfillers yet. Four things you saw, in your words: it shows table names where people
expect display names ("on the ServiceNow home page it directly says `ui_page`"); it gives results without evaluating the user's access
levels; knowledge articles open "sometimes in the native view, sometimes in the portal view, like that etc."; and, later in the day,
"I opened ifBash.com in Chrome and Agent Buddy is occupying space for it". You asked for everything to be found and fixed, with as many
agents as needed. This page says what was found, what fixed each part, what the real instance answered, and what is still yours to decide.
Nothing is committed or pushed: the work is in the working tree of `D:\AgentBuddy`.

## How it was done

- **Audit (read-only):** nine reviews in parallel, one per area: raw names on the screens; what the panel does on a page that is not a
  ticket; read-side access; write-side access and roles; what ServiceNow supports for a non-admin session (a web research); every address
  the panel builds (links); does each record type get what its worker needs; every sentence the product says (jargon); and feature parity
  across classic, Next Experience, Workspaces and portals. About 160 findings, many of them the same cause seen from different screens.
  The reports are in `docs/review/audit-2026-11-07/` (one file each, with the VERIFIED / ASSUMED mark on every claim).
- **Build:** four waves of builders working on separate files (`docs/build-rules.md`: each owns its files, nobody commits, only safe checks
  in parallel), each wave integrated by the lead with the whole gate and the end-to-end runs before the next began. 14 builders and 5
  integration runs in all.
- **Live checks:** read-only requests on the real instance `dev364920`, as the administrator, as **ITIL User** (direct role `itil` only)
  and as **Abel Tuter** (no role), reached through the normal "Impersonate user" menu and ended afterwards. Results:
  `docs/live-checks-2026-11-07.md`. They changed two decisions and settled about a dozen of the audit's open questions.

## What the live checks answered

Full table with the measured values: `docs/live-checks-2026-11-07.md`. In short:

- `ui_page` is the home page's UI Page, for every user.
- A field the user cannot read arrives as a **missing key**, not an empty string; a row-filtered read is `200` with no rows while
  `X-Total-Count` shows the unfiltered total; a table the role cannot read is `403 User Not Authorized`, an ordinary role rule.
- `g_user` exists in the classic form frame and nowhere in Next Experience or the Workspace: roles are unknown there.
- A closed form reports a real, shorter list of editable fields (not an empty one).
- A plain agent cannot read `sys_choice` but can read each column's labels and choices from `/api/now/ui/meta/<table>`; `itil` inherits
  the role that opens `sys_documentation` and `sys_db_object` (not tested for a fulfiller without `itil`).
- `kb_view.do` opens the article reader without a redirect (`enable_redirect` is false here), also for a user with no role.
- The real state values for incident, problem, change, tasks and requests, and the real Workspace composer (two boxes named
  `work_notes` and `comments`, buttons "Post Work notes" and "Post Comments").

## Three roles to Strong (wave 6)

After the audit the Fulfiller Value Map (https://claude.ai/artifact/Gv53v2EF2HSCdEt5W7y5Dv) rated the catalog fulfiller, the team lead and the
knowledge manager Good. They were built toward Strong (nothing was committed; the ifBash copy was not touched):

- **Catalog fulfiller:** the card "Fulfilled before" (the last completed tasks of the same catalog item, with close notes and how long they took),
  the comment wording from who opened the task (the platform does not guarantee that a requester cannot read a catalog task's comments),
  and "Waiting on X for N days" on the Request card.
- **Team lead:** "Needs attention now" first on the Team overview, six sections open and nine folded, the counts proven equal to the sections
  they come from, and the inconsistencies between sections fixed.
- **Knowledge manager:** "Compared N of TOTAL articles in SCOPE", "Compare the next 300" on a large knowledge base, a line on why each
  duplicate group was grouped, and the number of tickets behind each gap topic. Expiring and stale articles are ServiceNow's own dashboards
  (`docs/agent-pain-points.md`, "Platform recipes").

**Evidence still needed before any of the three is Strong:** a pilot with a real catalog fulfiller, a team lead and a knowledge manager; a
knowledge base with more than 1,500 articles (one scan stops there and says so); and sample catalog data on dev364920 (nothing was created:
the owner has not yet allowed creating records there). The open decision stays open: whether per-person Team rows are for managers only.
Live questions: `docs/live-questions/audit-1107-catalog.md`, `audit-1107-lead.md` and `audit-1107-km.md`.

## Yours to decide

1. **`docs/states.md`:** the meaning of every state, one row per table and value, and what each screen does with it. The table follows
   ServiceNow's own values; where the old classifiers disagreed the choice is written down with its reason.
2. **The article link rule:** an agent opening an article gets the reader by article number; the copied link is the same single address
   whatever tab the agent is on (`kb_view.do`); the Duplicates screen keeps the authoring form. One table in `src/lib/snLinks.js` holds
   the portal page ids, so a different answer is a one-line change.
3. **The catalog-task comment channel:** work note first, the comment second with "the requester may not see this".
4. **The toolbar icon:** it no longer closes an open panel (use the X in the panel's header); on a page that is not added it opens
   Settings > ServiceNow sites; on an unadded `*.service-now.com` address it opens the panel so "Turn on for this site" works.
5. **`sc_request` "Pending Approval":** its stored value is `requested`, its label says it is waiting; the label decides on this site.
6. **Per-person Team rows (W-14):** today every member of a group sees each member's workload; what a member may see is decided by
   the platform's own read rules, not by a role in the panel. You may want per-person rows for the group's manager only.
7. **What the AI is told the agent can do (W-16):** the AI prompts carry no capability information (for example that the form has no
   Resolve option); low value, and prompts must stay fixed text. Not built.
