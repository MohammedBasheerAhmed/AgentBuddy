# Live questions: Arabic, right-to-left, shortcuts and the self-hosted install

Things the build could not check without a real person, a real keyboard or a real managed computer. Each one: what was assumed, how to
check it as an ordinary user, and what to change if it is wrong. Answer them in the V1 walk-through.

## 1. Does the Arabic read well?

- **Assumed:** Modern Standard Arabic, the words in `docs/i18n-arabic.md` (حادثة, طلب, مجموعة الإسناد ...). Nobody who uses Arabic ServiceNow
  has read it yet; it was written by the model that built the panel.
- **Check:** switch Settings > Language to العربية, open a ticket, walk Ticket, My work, Team, Write, Knowledge and Settings. Note any
  text that is stiff, wrong for a support team, or different from the word your ServiceNow uses.
- **If wrong:** the texts are in `src/i18n/ar/*.js`, one line per English text; the glossary in `docs/i18n-arabic.md` says which word to
  use. Send the corrections as a list "English text -> better Arabic".

## 2. Is the right-to-left layout right in the real side panel?

- **Assumed:** the browser's side panel is a normal page whose `dir="rtl"` turns the layout; the end-to-end run checks this in a popup
  window with the same page, at 440 px and 320 px wide, in light and dark.
- **Check:** open the real side panel in Chrome and in Edge with Arabic on. Look at: the order of the screen tabs (Ticket first, on the
  right), the Insights / AI Assist switch, the arrows on the cards (folded: pointing left), the tables on Team, the preview dialog.
- **If wrong:** `src/ui/areas/zz-rtl.css` holds what is specific to right-to-left; everything else uses logical properties.

## 3. Is the page bar right in Arabic?

- **Assumed:** the dock on the ServiceNow page takes its language from the same setting. In Arabic the mark sits at the right end of the
  dock and the icons run right to left; the tooltips are in Arabic.
- **Check:** with Arabic on, open a ticket in ServiceNow: hover the mark and each icon (the name, the AI mark, the line saying where
  text goes), click the mark ("Close the Agent Buddy quick actions"). Does the order of the icons feel right?

## 4. A ServiceNow whose own language is Arabic

- **Assumed:** some checks read the DISPLAYED value of a field and compare it with English words (`closed`, `resolved`, `cancelled` in
  `src/context/history.js`, `src/chat/stats.js`, `src/checks/states.js`, `src/compose/emailRecommend.js`, `src/insights/glance.js`).
  If an agent's ServiceNow itself shows Arabic (their user language is Arabic), those values are Arabic and the English patterns do
  not match: a closed ticket could be shown as open, an email suggestion could be wrong.
- **Check:** as an agent whose ServiceNow language is Arabic, open a closed incident, a cancelled request and a resolved case; look at
  the card "Ticket journey", the "Which email fits" card, and whether "Ready to resolve?" says the right thing.
- **If wrong:** compare the numeric `value` of the state (`sysparm_display_value=all` returns both) instead of the displayed words. Tell
  us which cards were wrong and we will do that for each.

## 5. AI answers in Arabic

- **Assumed:** the prompts already say "write in the language of the ticket"; the new setting "Language of AI answers" adds a fixed
  sentence that overrides it ("the language of the panel"). The model you use writes good Arabic.
- **Check:** with DeepSeek (or your model): summarise an Arabic ticket and an English one; draft a reply to an Arabic customer; choose
  "The language of the panel" with Arabic on, and summarise an English ticket. Check that names, record numbers and masked values
  (`[PERSON_1]`) come back intact inside the Arabic sentences, and that Insert puts the text into the right field.
- **Also:** masking in Arabic text (Arabic digits, Arabic labels, names with different spellings) is tested on made-up tickets
  (`tests/pii-arabic.test.js`); read a real Arabic ticket's preview and tell us anything that is still visible and should not be.

## 6. The keyboard shortcuts

- **Assumed:** `Alt+Shift+1` opens the panel, `Alt+Shift+2` summarises, `Alt+Shift+3` drafts a reply (Mac: `Control+Shift+1/2/3`); they
  work with an English or an Arabic keyboard layout active (digits, so no letter that moves), and they do not clash with the browser or
  ServiceNow. The side panel can only be opened while a key press is being handled; the code opens it first.
- **Check:** in Chrome and in Edge, on Windows and on a Mac, with the Arabic layout and the English layout active: press each. Also
  `chrome://extensions/shortcuts` (`edge://extensions/shortcuts`) shows the three and lets you change them.
- **If wrong:** the keys are in `manifest.json` (`commands`); the browser keeps a person's own choice across updates.
- **Other browsers:** Edge `edge.md` 13 and 14; Firefox `firefox.md` 8 and 17 (Firefox changes them at `about:addons`, the gear,
  Manage Extension Shortcuts).

## 7. The self-hosted install

- **Assumed:** `scripts/selfhost.js` makes a package the browsers accept (it matches a package made by Chrome's own packer), and the policy
  files with the extension id and update address install and update it on a managed computer.
- **Check:** follow "Self-hosted install" in `docs/deployment.md` with two pilot computers, one Windows (Chrome and Edge), one Mac.
  Then raise the version, pack again, upload, and see the update arrive.
- **If wrong:** note what `chrome://policy` and `chrome://extensions` say (an error text, "not allowed", a policy that shows an error).

## 8. Look-alike search in Arabic: how ServiceNow matches Arabic text (round 4)

- **Assumed:** the "contains" search (`LIKE` in an encoded query) on the short description is a plain substring match on the text as
  it was stored, and the stored text is not folded: "بريد" finds "البريد" and "بريدي", but "إعادة" (with hamza) does not find a ticket
  that wrote "اعادة", and "كلمة" does not find "كلمه". So the panel searches for each word exactly as the caller wrote it (only an
  article such as "ال" or "بال" in front, and an ending such as "ي", "ة", "ات" on a long word, are left off: the rest is still a piece of
  every form), and only the ranking in the panel compares the folded spellings (`src/lib/similar.js`).
- **Check (no admin needed):** in the incident list, filter "Short description contains بريد" and see that tickets with "البريد" and
  "بريدي" show. Then pick a ticket written with "إعادة" and one with "اعادة": filter "contains إعاد". If both show, the database folds
  Arabic letters, and the search could be wider; if only the first shows, it is as assumed.
- **If wrong:** tell us what was found; the query form is `similarKeywords` and `queryTerm` in `src/lib/similar.js`.

## 9. Arabic in the text of knowledge articles

- **Assumed:** the body of an Arabic article is stored as Arabic characters in its HTML, not as character codes (`&#1576;`), so "text
  contains <word>" finds it.
- **Check:** open an Arabic article and pick a word that is only in its body, not its title. Search for it in the Knowledge screen's
  search, and in ServiceNow's own article list with "Text contains <word>". If only the title search finds articles, the body is stored as
  codes.
- **If wrong:** tell us; the body search would need another way to match.

## 10. Short vowels and the stretched line (tatweel) in tickets

- **Assumed:** callers rarely write short vowels (مُشكلة) or stretch words (البـريد). The search words drop them, so a ticket written with
  them finds tickets written without. The other way round (the stored ticket has them, the new one does not) cannot match.
- **Check:** if your tickets often carry short vowels or the stretched line, tell us; we would then also search a second spelling.

## 11. Endings taken off Arabic search words

- **Assumed:** cutting a common ending off a word of five letters or more (حسابي → حساب, طابعة → طابع, الطابعات → طابع) finds more of
  the right look-alikes than wrong ones.
- **Check:** on five Arabic tickets, open "Seen this before?" and judge whether the look-alikes are about the same thing.
- **If wrong:** name the words that pulled in unrelated tickets; the list is `ARABIC_ENDINGS` in `src/lib/similar.js`.

## 12. The Arabic word lists of the no-AI rules

- **Assumed:** the lists cover how your customers and teams write: Modern Standard Arabic and a few everyday words from every dialect
  (زهقت, زعلان). Risk and urgency: `ARABIC_WORDS` in `src/itsm/sentiment.js`. Missing details (who else is affected, the error message
  or a screenshot, the steps, a vague title): `ARABIC_RULES` in `src/insights/missing.js`. Checklist action words (imperatives and verbal
  nouns: أنشئ / إنشاء): `AR_VERBS` in `src/checks/checklist.js`. "Since when" has no rule, in English or in Arabic.
- **Check:** on five real Arabic tickets compare the Risk and Missing details lines with your own reading; on two Arabic catalog tasks
  compare the checklist with the written instructions. Note what was missed and what fired for nothing.
- **If wrong:** send the words or sentences; each list is data, one line per label.

## 13. Languages other than English and Arabic

- **Assumed:** look-alikes and knowledge search work for any language written with spaces between words (French, Spanish, German,
  Russian, Hebrew...), without a list of filler words for them, so with more noise. Chinese, Japanese, Thai and the other scripts written
  without spaces are not supported: their letters are left out of the search. The risk, missing-details and checklist rules read English
  and Arabic only.
- **Check:** tell us which other languages your tickets come in, and how often.

## 14. The language of the no-AI email template

- **Assumed:** the template (Email card) is written in the customer's language: by default the language the customer writes in (their
  newest comment, else the short description and description), the same rule as "Language of AI answers: The language of the ticket".
  The panel's language alone never changes it. With "The language of the panel" chosen, the panel's language is used once the Email
  card passes that setting (`emailLanguage` in `src/insights/emailTemplates.js`).
- **Check:** an Arabic caller's ticket shows the Arabic template; an English ticket with the panel in Arabic shows the English one. Read
  the Arabic templates: is the tone right for your customers ("مرحبا أحمد،", "مع أطيب التحيات،"), or do you write "السيد/السيدة" and
  "تحية طيبة وبعد"?
- **If wrong:** the texts are `EMAIL_TEMPLATES_AR` in `src/insights/emailTemplates.js`.
